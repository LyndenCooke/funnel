AI CPD for Schools: Training That Teachers Actually Use

Most school AI training ends with a round of polite applause and a folder of slides that nobody opens again. There is a better way to build AI confidence across your staff.

Every year, schools across the UK spend thousands of pounds on AI CPD. The format is almost always the same: a consultant flies in, delivers a presentation full of impressive slides about what AI could do, and leaves before lunch. Teachers return to their classrooms that afternoon feeling overwhelmed. By Monday morning, nothing has changed. The slides sit in a shared drive. The AI tools remain untouched. The budget is spent.

It does not have to be this way. Effective AI professional development looks nothing like a one-off training day. It looks like a community.

Why Most AI CPD Fails

1

Delivered by consultants, not teachers

The person leading your AI training has probably never planned a lesson using AI. They have never written a set of report cards with it, or used it to differentiate a worksheet for five ability levels in twenty minutes. They know AI in theory. They do not know AI in the classroom. That gap matters more than most schools realise.

2

A one-off event, not ongoing support

A single CPD session cannot build a new skill. Teachers need repeated practice, feedback, and time to experiment. One afternoon is not enough. Learning AI is like learning any other professional skill: it requires consistency, not intensity. Without follow-up, even the best session fades within a week.

3

All theory, no practice

Too many AI sessions are spectator events. Teachers watch someone else use AI on a screen. They do not open a tool themselves. They do not write a prompt. They do not build anything. When the session ends, they have seen what is possible but have no idea how to do it themselves.

70% of teachers do not feel confident using AI tools. That number does not change after a single CPD session.

What Effective AI CPD Looks Like

If your school is serious about building AI capability, look for these five characteristics in any CPD programme you consider.

Led by practitioners, not vendors

The trainer should be someone who uses AI in a real classroom, not someone selling a product. Practitioners understand the constraints teachers face: limited time, changing curricula, mixed-ability classes, and the reality that not everything AI produces is usable without editing.

Ongoing, not one-off

Weekly touchpoints beat annual events. Teachers need space to try something, come back with questions, and refine their approach. A drip-feed model builds lasting habits. A firehose model builds frustration.

Practical and task-specific

Teachers should leave each session with something they can use the next day. Not a vague understanding of large language models, but a working prompt for writing assessment feedback or a template for generating differentiated resources.

Differentiated

Not every teacher is at the same level. Some have never opened ChatGPT. Others are already building custom workflows. Good AI CPD recognises this range and provides a structured progression, from Explorer to Practitioner to Integrator to Builder to Native, so everyone has a clear next step.

Community-supported

Teachers need a place to ask questions, share wins, and get help when they get stuck. A community of peers at different stages creates a support network that outlasts any single training day. The staffroom conversation about AI should not end when the consultant leaves.

How to Use Run AI for Your School

You do not need a formal rollout plan. Here is how schools are getting started.

1

Have interested staff join the free community individually. There is no sign-up process for schools. Teachers join on their own, at no cost, using their personal or school email.

2

Use the AI Readiness Audit to baseline your team. Have each teacher complete the two-minute audit. This gives you a clear picture of where your staff are and where the gaps sit.

3

Set a goal. Something specific and achievable, like "everyone reaches Level 2 (AI Practitioner) by half-term." A shared target gives the initiative momentum and makes progress visible.

4

Share prompt libraries and workflows in your staff shared drive. As teachers build prompts and workflows inside Run AI, encourage them to save and share what works. Over time, your school builds its own AI resource bank, tailored to your curriculum and context.
