AI Tools for Primary Teachers: A Practical Guide for KS1 and KS2

Honest recommendations from a working primary teacher. No hype, no jargon, no affiliate links.

There Are Too Many AI Tools. Here Is What Actually Works.

If you have searched for AI tools for primary teachers recently, you will know the feeling. There are hundreds of options, most of them American, most of them aimed at secondary, and most of them promising the world while delivering very little that works in a real KS1 or KS2 classroom.

I have spent the past year testing AI tools alongside my own teaching. Some of them have genuinely changed how I plan and assess. Others were a complete waste of time. This guide is a curated, honest look at the tools that actually work for primary teachers in the UK. No affiliate links. No sponsored recommendations. Just what I would tell a colleague over a cup of tea in the staffroom.

What to Look for in an AI Tool

Before you try anything, it helps to know what separates a useful AI tool from a shiny distraction. Here is what I look for:

  • Free or genuinely affordable. Most of us are not spending our own money on subscriptions. The tool needs a usable free tier or cost less than a takeaway coffee each month.
  • No technical skills required. If it needs a login process more complicated than signing up for a streaming service, most teachers will not bother. And rightly so.
  • UK curriculum alignment. American grade levels and standards are not helpful when you are planning for the Year 4 maths curriculum.
  • Saves time on tasks you actually do. Flashy features are meaningless if they do not help with planning, assessment, differentiation, or communication.

One thing worth saying upfront: every AI tool needs a human to check and refine the output. None of them produce lesson-ready material straight away. Think of AI as a very fast first draft, not a finished product.

The Best Free AI Tools for Primary Teachers

ChatGPT (Free Tier)

Free / Best for: open-ended planning, differentiation, parent letters

ChatGPT remains the most versatile tool in the box. The free tier gives you access to GPT-4o, which is more than capable of drafting lesson plans, generating differentiation ideas, writing parent communication letters, and brainstorming cross-curricular links. It is not specifically designed for teachers, which means you need to give it good context in your prompts. But once you learn to do that, it becomes incredibly powerful.

Here is an example prompt I use regularly for KS2 maths planning:

Example prompt for KS2 maths "I am a Year 5 teacher following the English National Curriculum. I need to plan a week of maths lessons on fractions, decimals, and percentages. Each lesson should last 60 minutes and include a warm-up activity, direct teaching input, guided practice, and an independent task. Please differentiate for three groups: working towards, expected, and greater depth. Include concrete, pictorial, and abstract approaches."

Oak AI (Aila)

Free / Best for: curriculum-aligned lesson planning

Aila is Oak National Academy's AI lesson planning assistant, and it is completely free. What makes it stand out is that it draws from Oak's quality-assured content library, which is already aligned to the English National Curriculum. You tell it what you want to teach, and it builds a structured lesson plan with learning outcomes, key vocabulary, and suggested activities. For primary teachers who want something that works out of the box without wrestling with prompts, Aila is the best starting point.

Eduaide.Ai (Free Tier)

Free / Best for: assessment creation and pedagogically sound resources

Eduaide offers over 150 resource generators built on strong pedagogical foundations. It is particularly good for creating assessments, comprehension questions, and formative checks. The free tier is generous enough for daily use, and the interface is straightforward. If you find yourself spending hours writing end-of-unit assessments, Eduaide will give you a solid starting point in minutes.

Canva Magic Write

Free with Canva for Education / Best for: classroom displays and visual resources

If you already use Canva for displays and worksheets, Magic Write adds AI-assisted text generation directly inside your designs. It is useful for creating classroom posters with age-appropriate explanations, generating text for learning walls, or drafting instructions for display. It is not a planning tool, but it saves real time on the visual side of classroom preparation.

Best Paid Tools Worth Considering

Not every school has budget for AI subscriptions, and that is fine. The free tools above will serve you well. But if you or your school are ready to invest, these are the paid options that offer genuine value.

MagicSchool AI

$12.99/month / Best for: schools wanting one platform for everything

MagicSchool offers over 80 tools covering lesson planning, assessment, IEP writing, communication, and more. It is the widest ranging platform available, and it works well as a whole-school solution where you want every teacher on the same system. The individual price is steep for a single teacher, but the school pricing makes it more reasonable if your leadership team is on board.

Eduaide Pro

$5.99/month / Best for: strongest pedagogy focus at an affordable price

The paid tier of Eduaide unlocks all 150+ generators without limits. At under six pounds a month, it is the most affordable paid option with genuine pedagogical depth. If you are a teacher who cares about the quality of your resources and wants AI that understands how learning works, this is the one to try first.

Diffit

Free tier available, paid plans from $9.99/month / Best for: differentiated reading materials

Diffit is specifically excellent at one thing: creating differentiated reading materials across ability levels. You give it a topic or paste in a text, and it generates versions at different reading levels, complete with vocabulary support and comprehension questions. For primary teachers juggling mixed-ability classes, this can save hours of differentiation work every week.

A Simple Way to Get Started

The biggest mistake I see teachers make is trying to use five tools at once and then giving up after a week. Do not do that. Instead, pick one tool and one task. That is it.

For example: use ChatGPT to draft your weekly maths planning for one week. Just one subject, one week. See how it goes. Refine the output. Notice where it saves you time and where it does not. Then decide whether to keep going or try a different tool for a different task.

What I Wish Someone Had Told Me

AI is a starting point, not a finished product. You will spend less time creating from scratch and more time refining what AI gives you. That is the trade-off, and it is a good one.

The real skill is not finding the right tool. It is learning to prompt well. The better you get at telling AI exactly what you need, including the year group, the curriculum objectives, the ability levels, and the format, the better the output becomes. It feels clumsy at first. It gets easier with practice, and much faster than you expect.

Do not wait until you feel ready. Start with something small, learn as you go, and give yourself permission to get it wrong a few times. That is how every teacher I know has made AI work for them.
