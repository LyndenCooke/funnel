# Direct Response Copy Skill

Write copy that makes people act. Not copy that sounds clever.

## Core Principles
1. Lead with pain, then solve it.
2. Use "you" language. Never "our users" or "one might consider."
3. Be specific, not superlative. "Save 10 hours a week" beats "Transform your productivity."
4. The headline test: does it create an emotional response, or just describe the product?
5. The tired teacher test: would a Year 4 teacher on a Sunday evening stop scrolling for this?
6. CTAs are instructions, not labels. "Join the Free Community" not "Sign Up."

## British English (Non-Negotiable)
- ise not ize (specialise, organise, recognise)
- our not or (colour, behaviour, favourite)
- re not er (centre, metre) in copy. CSS values like color: are fine.
- programme not program (unless a computer program)
- No em dashes or en dashes. Commas, full stops, or restructure.
- No emojis.

## Forbidden Phrases
Never use: revolutionise, unlock your potential, game-changing, cutting-edge, leverage (verb), synergy, holistic, paradigm shift, next-level, best-in-class, world-class, seamless, empower, elevate, harness the power of, deep dive (noun).

## Pain Point Writing
Weak: "Lesson planning takes too long."
Strong: "You spent your entire Sunday afternoon on a set of Year 3 maths slides that ChatGPT could have drafted in 4 minutes."
