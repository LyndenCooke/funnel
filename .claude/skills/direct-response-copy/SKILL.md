# Direct Response Copy Skill

## Purpose
British English conversion copy for the Run AI project. All visible text must pass through this skill's guidelines.

## Language Rules

### British English (Non-Negotiable)
- **-ise not -ize:** organise, specialise, recognise, optimise
- **-our not -or:** colour, behaviour, favourite, honour
- **-re not -er:** centre, metre, theatre (but: computer, chapter)
- **-programme not program** (except computer program)
- **-ence not -ense:** licence (noun), defence, offence
- **-t not -ed:** learnt, spelt, dreamt (both acceptable, prefer -t)
- **-s not -z in specific words:** analyse, paralyse, catalyse
- Use "whilst" and "amongst" where natural (but "while" and "among" are also fine)
- "Maths" not "math"
- "Year 4" not "4th grade"
- "Key Stage 2" / "KS2" not "elementary"
- "SEND" not "special education" or "SPED"
- "Ofsted" not "school inspection"
- "CPD" not "professional development" (though spell it out on first use: "continuing professional development (CPD)")

### Forbidden Phrases
Never use these in any copy:
- "Game-changer" or "game changing"
- "Revolutionise" or "revolutionary"
- "Cutting-edge" or "bleeding-edge"
- "Next-level"
- "Unlock your potential"
- "Transform your [anything]"
- "Leverage" (as a verb)
- "Synergy" or "synergistic"
- "Deep dive" (as a noun)
- "Circle back"
- "Low-hanging fruit"
- "Move the needle"
- "Best-in-class"
- "World-class"
- "Empower" or "empowering"
- "Innovative" or "innovation" (unless describing a specific, named innovation)
- "Journey" when used as corporate jargon (acceptable when referring to the actual Run AI learning journey)
- "Seamless" or "seamlessly"
- "Robust"
- "Holistic"
- "Ecosystem"
- Any phrase that sounds like it came from a LinkedIn influencer's post

### Formatting Rules
- No emojis. Ever.
- No em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Use commas, full stops, or rewrite the sentence.
- No exclamation marks in body copy. One per page maximum in headlines, and only if genuinely warranted.
- Use "do not" instead of "don't" in formal copy. Contractions are fine in conversational copy (emails, community posts) but default to expanded forms for landing pages and ads.
- Numbers: spell out one to nine, use digits for 10 and above. Exception: always use digits in statistics and data points ("5 hours", "3 tools", "100+ teachers").

## Tone of Voice

### The Tired Teacher Test
Every piece of copy must pass this test: imagine a tired teacher at 8pm on a Sunday, halfway through marking, scrolling their phone. Would they:
1. **Stop scrolling?** The hook must interrupt their pattern.
2. **Keep reading?** The body must feel relevant to their actual life.
3. **Feel understood?** The copy must demonstrate that the writer knows what their week looks like.
4. **Take action?** The CTA must feel low-effort and high-reward.

If the answer to any of these is no, rewrite.

### Voice Characteristics
- **Warm, not corporate.** Write like a colleague in the staffroom, not a vendor in a sales pitch.
- **Direct, not aggressive.** State things plainly without being pushy or manipulative.
- **Practical, not theoretical.** Every claim should connect to something a teacher does in their actual day.
- **Honest, not hype-driven.** Acknowledge limitations. AI is a tool, not magic. It needs human oversight.
- **Specific, not vague.** "Save 10 hours a week" is better than "save time." "Year 4 maths planning" is better than "lesson planning."

### Addressing the Reader
- Use "you" and "your" directly.
- Refer to them as "teachers" (their identity), not "users" or "customers" or "educators" (too formal).
- When being specific: "primary teachers", "Year 2 teachers", "Heads of English", "CPD coordinators".
- Never: "dear educator", "fellow professional", "esteemed colleague".

### Lynden's Voice (First Person)
When writing as Lynden:
- Warm, slightly self-deprecating, honest about his own learning process.
- "I am not an AI expert. I am a teacher who got curious."
- References his actual teaching experience (Reception to Year 6, British curriculum, EAL, SEN).
- Shares what he is building, including the messy parts.
- Never positions himself as a guru. Always as a guide who is one step ahead.

## Copy Structures

### Headlines
- Lead with the benefit or the pain point, not the product.
- Good: "Teaching Is Hard Enough. Let AI Handle the Rest."
- Bad: "Run AI: The Premier AI Training Community for Educators."

### Body Copy
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences maximum).
- One idea per paragraph.
- Use sentence fragments for impact when appropriate: "No jargon. No gurus. Just practical guidance."

### CTAs
- Primary: Action verb + benefit. "Join the Free Community."
- Secondary: Lower commitment. "Take the AI Readiness Audit."
- Ghost/text: Curiosity-driven. "Find out where you stand."
- Never: "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" (too generic).

## Quality Checklist
Before publishing any copy:
- [ ] British English spelling throughout
- [ ] No forbidden phrases
- [ ] No emojis, em dashes, or en dashes
- [ ] Passes the tired teacher test
- [ ] Specific, not vague
- [ ] One clear message per section
- [ ] CTA is clear and action-oriented
- [ ] Tone is warm and practical, not corporate or hype-driven
